How the spots are chosen

Most notes on the dulcimer can be struck in two or three places. For each note the page picks the spot that keeps each hand's moves short and keeps your hands from crossing, looking at the whole tune at once. It is a sensible first draft, not a rule. When you would play a note somewhere else, select it and use the other spot.

Alternation

Counting long notes keeps both hands on the eighth-note pulse. A quarter note takes up two strokes, the second one silent, so the lead hand always falls on the beat and both passes of a repeat are hammered the same way. Every note played alternates strictly, so long notes don't count.

Backup

The backup follows the tune's chord symbols when it has them. Otherwise the page suggests a chord for each half bar from the melody notes, favoring the home chord and keeping a chord when the melody allows. Bass and chord puts a bass note on the beat and a two-note chord off the beat. Shed rolls run root, third, fifth and octave in eighth notes. A seventh chord puts its seventh into both patterns, and a slash chord such as G/B puts its named note in the bass.

Harmony

The harmony note is struck with the melody note on each beat, by the other hand. Thirds and sixths follow the key, so they come out major or minor as the scale gives them. A fifth below is often straight across the treble bridge from the melody note.

The triangle

Every course to the left of the treble bridge sounds a fifth above the same course on the right. So a chord sits in a triangle: root on the right of the treble bridge, the third two courses up on the same side, the fifth straight across the bridge from the root.

The bass bridge works the same way one step down: root on the bass, third two courses up, fifth straight across on the right of the treble bridge.

The shed

Root, third, fifth and octave, split between your hands. The right hand plays a third: the root and the course two up. The left hand plays a fourth: the fifth straight across from the root, and the octave three courses above it. The root at the bottom right and the octave at the top left are the same note an octave apart.

It works across the valley, from the bass onto the right side of the treble, and across the treble bridge itself. The dashed outlines mark every place it fits for the chord you picked.

Major or minor

The triangle always gives you the chord that belongs to the scale. In the middle of the treble, start it on G, D, or C and it is major; start it on A, B, or E and it is minor. Near the top the F course changes things: the high D triangle gives D minor. You change chord quality by moving the shape, not by changing it.

The odd courses

Cloud Nine swaps a few courses out of the plain diatonic pattern. The bass has F and B♭ in its upper half, the treble has an F where you'd expect F♯, and the top course on each bridge is a chromatic extra (G♯ and D♯ on the treble, D♯ on the bass).

That's why F major works: its triangle sits on the bass, rooted on F with C straight across. The low A on the bass is a lone extra below the D, so it rarely fits a shape.
